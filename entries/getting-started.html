Communication
BRIEF COMPACT

Brief and compact set your display options for the game. Typing brief toggles on and off the showing of room descriptions when you move around (considerably reducing the text to be processed), and typing compact removes the extra blank line before your prompt.

DEAF

The deaf toggle turns off incoming tells, helpful if you are being bombarded by messages from other players. You can still use reply (see help reply) to reply to people who were telling you before you went deaf, although you cannot reply to a deaf person unless you or they are a deity (it's mostly useful for sending a quick "I'm being spammed so I went deaf" message to a person).

AFK

Typing afk puts your character in a tell-saving mode: any tell sent to you is stored in a special buffer and can be seen later by typing replay. This is useful when you need to leave the mud for a few minutes but don't want to miss tells. AFK shows up in your prompt until it is turned off. You may also type replay while AFK.

Tells to link-dead players are stored in a similar fashion, and can also be seen by typing replay when they reconnect (unless the character logs off before reading them).

REPLAY
replayShows all tells stored while you were AFK or link-dead. The buffer is cleared after you view it.

See also: help afk

TICK

Many of the game actions are based upon interval timers, including combat, most autonomous monster actions, hp/mana/move regeneration, spell duration, weather, and area resetting.

Of these timers, the hp/mana/move regeneration timer is popularly called the 'tick'. Ticks on this mud average 70 seconds of real time, but the actual amount varies randomly from 65 seconds to 75 seconds.

Area resetting happens roughly every 3 minutes if no one is in the area; less often (15 minutes) if someone is. Because areas take so long to reset if someone is in the area, it is generally considered bad etiquette to sleep in an area, or sit in a popular leveling area doing nothing. Also note that objects lying on the ground will not be regenerated if anyone is in the area when it resets.

CAST
cast <spell> <target>Casts a spell at a target. The target is optional — many spells use an appropriate default, especially during combat.

It is easier to cast a spell after you have practiced it. The more you practice, the higher chance you have of success. You may practice any spell up to 75%. Mana cost decreases as your level increases.

If the spell name is more than one word, you must quote it. Example: cast 'cure critic' target. Quoting is optional for single-word spells. You can abbreviate: c heal self

When you cast an offensive spell, the victim usually gets a saving throw. The effect is reduced or eliminated if the saving throw succeeds.

Levels
EXPERIENCE LEVEL XP

Your character advances in power by gaining experience. You need a set amount of experience points for each level based on your creation points.

You gain experience by:

  • Killing a creature on your own.
  • Being part of a group that kills a monster.
  • Getting better at a skill or spell.

You lose experience by:

  • Fleeing out of combat. (10 exp)
  • Recalling out of combat. (25 exp)
  • Being the target of energy drain spells.
  • Dying.

The experience you get from a kill depends on several things: how many players are in your group; your level versus the level of the monster; your alignment versus the monster's alignment; how many of this monster have been killed lately; your total hours; and some random variation.

Once you level, several things happen. You gain hit points, mana points, and practices depending on various factors. You also gain movement points and one training session. Your experience until next level is reset. You will also be able to wear new equipment and gain access to new skills and spells.

PRACTICE
practiceShows your current ability level in all skills and spells available to you. Can be used anywhere.
practice <skill|spell>Practices a skill or spell. Must be at a guild master to use.

Practice raises a skill up to a cap, after which it can only improve further through use in combat or casting. Most races and classes cap at 75%; Humans cap at 85%. Reclasses who specialize in a skill can exceed 100%, up to 130%.

The higher your Intelligence, the more you learn per session. The higher your Wisdom, the more practice sessions you gain per level. Unused sessions are saved until you use them.

See also: help specialize

TRAIN
train <str|int|wis|dex|con|hp|mana>Spends one training session to increase the chosen attribute, or to increase hp or mana by 10.

TRAIN increases one of your attributes. When you start the game, your character has standard attributes based on your class, and several initial training sessions. You can increase your attributes by using these sessions at a trainer (there are several in town). It takes one training session to improve an attribute, or to increase mana or hp by 10. You receive one session per level.

The best attributes to train first are WIS and CON. WIS gives you more practices when you gain a level. CON gives you more hit points. In the long run, your character will be most powerful if you train WIS and CON both before practicing or training anything else.

Dragons and slivers have additional train options. See also: help 'train dragon', help 'train sliver'.

Pets & Charmed Mobs
PET PETS

You can buy pets in the pet shop. You may own only one pet at a time. After the old one dies you may purchase a replacement. Your pet will follow you everywhere you go and help you in your adventures.

ORDER
order <mob> <command>Orders one of your charmed followers or pets to perform any command.
order all <command>Orders all charmed followers and pets to perform the command simultaneously.

You are responsible for the actions of your followers. Others who attack your followers incur the same penalty as if they attacked you directly. All charmed creatures lose their aggressive nature while charmed. If you charm a creature and then attack it, the charm will break. The more charmed creatures under your control, the harder it is to maintain control over them.

MACROBASICS

A macro is an extended alias with variables. Unlike aliases, macros support multiple commands separated by semicolons, and they can call other macros (including themselves). When a macro is running, any commands typed by the player take precedence over the macro's commands.

Commands in a macro can contain variables identified as %0 through %9. Arguments typed after the macro name fill the variables in order.

Example — define a macro that recalls and buys food:

macro define food recall; s; s; w; n; buy 10*pie

Example — define a grats macro with a variable:

macro define congrat grats Congratulations, %0, on your level!!!

Then typing congrat KillerBob sends: grats Congratulations, KillerBob, on your level!!!

Quest Master
QUEST MASTER

The quest master is a mob that distributes quests on request. Level 51+ questers are awarded cash and quest/experience points. From level 1 to 50, successfully completed quests receive the standard reward with a lessened amount of quest points. Quest points can be turned in for special equipment and other designated opportunities.

You must wait 8–12 minutes between solo quests (item or villain), or 15–20 minutes between group assault quests.

There are three kinds of quests: item quests (retrieve an item), villain quests (kill a monster), and assault quests (you and a group kill multiple monsters in an area). When typing quest request you can specify item, villain, or assault to get that kind of quest.

RPTITLE TITLE RECOGNITION ROLE PLAY

Recognition titles are titles which appear before your name, but after your clan affiliation on the who list. They are always used to recognize a social status, accomplishment, or title, and can be awarded in the following ways:

1. Ask an Immortal to evaluate your claim — usually through a quest more strenuous than typical quests.

2. Prove through a petition or list that you are commonly recognized by a given title. The Immortal will evaluate and either announce your new title or decline your claim.

3. Most recognition titles are unique. To claim a held title, a challenge must be issued simultaneously by note to both the Immortal and the current title holder.

All recognition titles require role-play justification. Retaining a title is an honour — every action of the bearer should reflect it. Titles which no longer apply will be removed. They can also be lost by failing to defend a challenge, holding an inappropriate title, or failing to live up to the title for an extended period.

Guidelines: Recognition titles should ideally read before your name — short, usually one or two words. Weaving your title subtly into your character's personality is highly recommended.
